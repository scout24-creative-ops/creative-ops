# Presentations for Jorin

Use these rules only for presentations prepared specifically for Jorin. Do not treat them as general presentation guidance.

## Goal

Make presentations quickly understandable, clearly prioritized and easy to retell. They should work with minimal verbal explanation.

Focus each presentation on four questions:

1. What are we working on?
2. Why is it relevant for Marketing & Communications?
3. What is Dominik's or Creative Operations' contribution?
4. What is the current status or next step?

## Core Principle

Treat the presentation more like an internal magazine or newspaper than a classic strategy or process deck.

Use:

- strong, memorable headlines
- short body copy
- few, clear bullets
- visible value for Marketing & Communications
- one core idea per slide

Avoid:

- technical deep dives
- long derivations or background stories
- dense process diagrams
- internal tool detail unless strategically necessary
- operational task lists
- political background or people assessments

## Recommended Slide Logic

A strong slide usually contains:

- **Eyebrow or label:** short classification such as `AI Workflow`, `Contentful Migration`, `AI Project Enablement` or `Current Team Check`
- **Headline:** a strong, understandable statement rather than a technical title
- **Body copy:** one or two sentences explaining context and relevance
- **Bullets:** two to four short points on progress, result or next step

Prefer headlines such as:

- `One brand. One language.`
- `Marketing gets the direct path to Contentful.`
- `Communication that matches the user's interest.`
- `Where does Marketing & Communications really stand with AI?`

Avoid technical titles such as:

- `Tone-of-Voice-Agent Setup`
- `Contentful MVP Process`
- `AI Workflow Architecture`

## Language and Tone

Use language that is:

- clear
- short
- confident
- non-technical
- understandable without specialist knowledge
- concrete about progress and next steps

Prefer active, visible formulations such as:

- `Strategic guidance for the team...`
- `Built a workflow that enables Marketing & Communications...`
- `Guided the team from idea to first usable pilot...`
- `Developed and presented the concept and roadmap...`
- `Next step...`

Avoid:

- long sentences
- passive wording without a clear contribution
- too many acronyms or tool names
- `I have...` in every line
- claiming operational ownership when Dominik's role is strategic or enabling

## Show Dominik's Contribution Correctly

Make Dominik's role visible without overstating ownership.

When Dominik is not the operational owner, emphasize:

- strategic guidance
- structure
- enablement
- connecting teams
- concept and roadmap work

Do not imply that Dominik performs the technical or operational implementation unless this is explicitly true.

## Appropriate Level of Detail

Condense complex project information into a simple, retellable message.

Do not explain the full project file. Show instead:

- what matters
- what is visible
- how it contributes to Marketing & Communications
- what Jorin can retell internally
- what happens next

Example:

Avoid:

> The workflow connects triggers, user signals, dynamic modules, landing-page generation and CMS integration.

Prefer:

> Marketing & Communications can address users according to their specific interest, using automatically created emails and landing pages.

## Recommended Deck Structure

For a short update:

1. Title and overall framing
2. Three to five current projects or themes, usually one slide per topic
3. Optional cross-project pattern or insight
4. Next step or needed decision
5. Simple closing slide

Limit project overviews to about five themes. More usually feels like an inventory instead of a prioritization.

## Visual Direction

Aim for:

- large headlines
- little text
- clear content areas
- bold visual elements
- one central idea per slide
- no dense tables or bullet walls

The target is neither classic PowerPoint nor a pure dashboard. Use bold editorial content surfaces with a large title, brief explanation and visual support.

## Quality Check

Before sharing, verify every slide:

- Can Jorin understand it in 30 to 60 seconds?
- Can he retell the point internally?
- Is the value for Marketing & Communications clear?
- Is Dominik's or Creative Operations' contribution visible?
- Is the next step clear?
- Does the slide work without verbal explanation?

If not, simplify the slide. It is probably too technical, abstract or process-heavy.

## Short Formula

`Strong headline. Short context. Clear value. Visible contribution. Concrete next step.`
