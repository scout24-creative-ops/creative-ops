# AI Enablement

## Purpose

Establish a practical, binding AI development format for Marketing & Communications that helps colleagues recognize useful AI opportunities, turn them into workable solutions and build confidence through real projects rather than generic training alone.

The project combines the completed AI Team-Check as its discovery and evidence phase with the ongoing AI Enablement Series as the practical response. The goal is to move from many individual experiments toward a shared learning and working structure with better orientation, visibility, practical support and reusable solutions.

## Project Phases

### Phase 1: AI Team-Check — completed discovery phase

Dominik conducted interviews across Marketing & Communications to understand how AI is currently used, where teams differ in experience, what already works and which recurring barriers prevent broader impact.

The planned interview phase is complete. The findings are not intended to rank teams or individuals. They provide the evidence base for the Enablement Series.

Core findings:

- AI is already used broadly, but with very different levels of depth and routine.
- There is no fundamental acceptance problem; curiosity and willingness to use AI are generally high.
- Many useful agents, workflows and experiments already exist, but they are often decentralized and not visible enough across teams.
- Recurring barriers include lack of time, missing orientation, limited visibility of existing solutions, uneven experience in moving from an idea to a reliable first solution, and quality or reliability problems.
- Not every process benefits from AI; better use-case selection and conscious decisions against unnecessary automation are part of AI maturity.
- Teams need practical guidance, examples, support in reducing ideas to small testable steps, shared learning and protected time more than a single generic basic training.

The Team-Check results are being refined into a concise visual landing page. They will be used both in leadership alignment and later as part of the first Enablement session.

### Phase 2: Series concept and preparation — current

The immediate focus is to turn the Team-Check findings into a clear, lightweight Enablement model and the supporting communication artifacts.

The current proposed series structure is:

1. A 45-minute kick-off with strategic framing by Jorin, selected AI Team-Check findings, orientation on how AI is changing work and a clear explanation of the series.
2. A 45-minute session `Vom Use Case zum Agent-Projekt`, focused on moving from a real work problem to a useful agent or workflow idea and a small testable first version, while introducing already prepared projects.
3. A recurring series combining one selected AI topic with short progress updates from every active person or group.

There is no separate final or consolidation session. Testing, learnings and continuation decisions are handled through the recurring project rhythm.

### Phase 3: AI Enablement Series — planned operating phase

The series should create a recurring shared structure for orientation, learning, project visibility and support while real development and testing continue in the teams between sessions.

## Target of the Series

The current simple formulation is:

`Kolleg:innen sollen lernen, aus konkreten Arbeitsproblemen sinnvolle AI-Lösungen zu entwickeln und umzusetzen. Die Serie gibt dafür Wissen, Orientierung und Unterstützung.`

The series should therefore help participants learn to think in useful AI solutions, apply transferable principles and build practical experience through real use cases.

## Working Model

- Every participating person or group works on a concrete agent or workflow that should create value for the relevant team or work context.
- Existing initiatives continue as real project work rather than being replaced by artificial workshop exercises.
- Colleagues without an existing idea can receive suitable suggestions based on real work problems.
- The actual development and testing happen mainly between the recurring sessions.
- In the recurring sessions, every active person or group gives a short update on progress, tests, findings, blockers and the next concrete step.
- Progress is not a competition or percentage score. Useful progress can include reducing scope, validating a blocker, rejecting an assumption or stopping an unhelpful direction.
- Existing solutions and experiences should become more visible so teams can learn from and reuse each other's work.

## Explanatory Landing Page

The current AI Enablement landing page should explain the series and create a simple shared understanding without becoming a working form or detailed curriculum.

The current content direction is intentionally simple:

- answer `Was ist ein AI-Agent?` in accessible everyday language;
- current core definition: `Ein AI-Agent ist ein Assistent, den wir einrichten können, damit er für uns eine wiederkehrende digitale Aufgabe übernimmt. Wir beschreiben dem Agenten die Aufgabe und stellen ihm die notwendigen Informationen und Tools zur Verfügung.`;
- use `Aufgabe`, `Informationen` and `Tools` as a simple mental model for what an agent needs;
- avoid overly technical architecture and process detail in the introduction;
- use the later six-step module to explain how an agent project is developed and tested, not to collect inputs;
- end with a call to action leading to the separate shared project presentation.

For the Teamlead alignment, the existing concept document is currently too text-heavy. The leadership version should focus on the overall model rather than detailed agendas for every session.

## Shared Project Presentation

The shared presentation is the working artifact for the appointment series and is separate from the explanatory landing page.

Its purpose is to collect only the information genuinely necessary for a clear project start and later updates. The current direction is:

1. A stable definition slide for the agent or workflow, including the problem, users, purpose and first MVP.
2. A recurring update slide showing meaningful developments since the last session, tests, learnings, blockers and the next step.

The exact fields, slide layout and handover process still need to be created.

## Decisions and Working Assumptions

- Treat the completed AI Team-Check as Phase 1 and the evidence base of the broader AI Enablement project, rather than as a separate active project.
- Use the Team-Check results to explain why the Enablement Series is needed and how it should be designed.
- Do not rank teams or individuals in the Team-Check results.
- Use a binding recurring format with leadership backing and protected time.
- Use two initial 45-minute sessions followed by a recurring series.
- Do not repeat a general shared agent-building workshop as a mandatory element.
- Start from real work problems and useful agents or workflows rather than building an agent for the sake of the format.
- Keep the recurring session structure simple: one focused learning topic followed by updates from active initiatives.
- Keep the working model lightweight and avoid unnecessary coordination overhead.
- Separate explanation from input: landing pages explain, while the shared presentation collects project information.
- Keep introductory explanations of AI agents simple and accessible.
- Current recommendation: use shared sessions primarily for orientation, learning, exchange, visibility, blocker discussion and next-step commitment; do most building and testing between sessions. This remains subject to leadership alignment.
